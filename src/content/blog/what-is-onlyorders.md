OnlyOrders is an AI Commerce Autopilot for conversational commerce. It is built for sellers who run their business inside chat on Messenger, WhatsApp, and Instagram, and it uses AI to handle the conversations that lead to a sale. This post is a clear, sourced overview of what the product does and why it is relevant to anyone thinking about AI application development.

## The core idea

OnlyOrders' own tagline is "Your orders, on autopilot." The product's pitch is that it turns DMs and comments into confirmed orders — handling smart replies, live catalog checks, payment verification, and courier handoff, all from one inbox.

The workflow is described in four steps on the site: Messenger AI replies answer price, size, and stock questions; checkout happens inside the chat; every order lands in one orders dashboard; and courier tracking is shared back to the buyer automatically. For a developer, this is a good concrete example of an agentic system in production: an AI assistant that takes actions (checking stock, confirming orders, arranging delivery) rather than just answering questions.

## Feature blocks

The OnlyOrders site lists four main feature blocks:

- **Instant replies** — AI answers customers 24/7.
- **One inbox** — every channel in one place.
- **Chat to order** — from receipt to courier handoff.
- **Auto posts** — drops published on schedule.

## Pricing

OnlyOrders publishes four tiers: Free ($0, 50 AI replies per month), Starter ($29, 500 replies per month), Pro ($79, 2,000 replies per month, badged "Most popular"), and Enterprise (custom). The Pro tier adds payment verification for bKash and Nagad plus courier integration. Yearly billing is advertised as saving up to 20%.

## Why it matters for AI developers

OnlyOrders is a useful case study because it combines several AI developer concepts covered on this blog: a conversational interface, tool use (checking a catalog, verifying payment), and guardrails around real-world actions. It shows how a relatively small AI application — a chat assistant wired to an order pipeline — can become the core of a business.

It also illustrates a fact we return to often: the hard part of an AI product is rarely the model. It is the integration, the evaluation, and the careful handling of actions with real consequences. OnlyOrders' design — containing the AI inside a defined order workflow — is a sensible pattern for that reason.
